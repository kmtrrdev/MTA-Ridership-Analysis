# 📊 NYC MTA Ridership Analysis

## 🚀 Overview

This project provides a comprehensive analysis of ridership trends across New York City's Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) system from **2019 to 2024**.

The analysis covers **7 major transportation modes**:

* 🚇 Subways
* 🚌 Buses
* 🚆 Long Island Rail Road (LIRR)
* 🚆 Metro-North Railroad
* ♿ Access-A-Ride (AAR)
* 🌉 Bridges & Tunnels
* 🚈 Staten Island Railway (SIR)

The objective is to evaluate the **impact of COVID-19**, measure **system recovery**, and uncover **behavioral changes in commuter patterns**.

---

## 🎯 Problem Statement

The COVID-19 pandemic caused a major disruption in public transportation systems worldwide.
This project aims to answer critical business questions:

* 📉 How severely did ridership decline?
* 📈 Which transportation modes recovered the fastest?
* 👥 How did user behavior change (Weekday vs Weekend vs Holiday)?
* 💰 Where should transportation authorities focus investments?

---

## 📊 Key Metrics

* **Total Ridership:** 11.24 Billion
* **Number of Years:** 6 (2019–2024)
* **Transport Modes:** 7

---

## 🧠 Key Insights (High-Impact 🔥)

### 1. 🚨 Severe COVID-19 Impact Across All Modes

* Ridership declined by **45% to 63%** across systems
* **Subways and Buses** experienced the largest absolute drops
* **Staten Island Railway (SIR)** was the most affected (~63%)

👉 **Insight:**
Remote work significantly reduced daily commuting demand.

---

### 2. 🚇 Subways Dominate the Entire System

* Total Subway ridership: **6.22 Billion**
* Represents the **largest share of total ridership**

👉 **Insight:**
Subways are the backbone of NYC transportation—any disruption here impacts the entire system.

---

### 3. 🚌 Buses Show Slower and Unstable Recovery

* Recovery peaked around **~65% (2022–2023)**
* Slight decline observed again in 2024

👉 **Insight:**
Bus users (often local commuters) have shifted behavior more permanently.

---

### 4. 🚆 Rail Systems Recover Gradually (LIRR & Metro-North)

* Steady improvement post-2020
* Still below full pre-pandemic levels

👉 **Insight:**
Long-distance commuters are returning slowly due to hybrid work models.

---

### 5. ♿ Access-A-Ride Fully Recovered (and Surpassed)

* Achieved **100%+ recovery** by 2023–2024

👉 **Insight:**
Essential transportation services are highly resilient to external shocks.

---

### 6. 🌉 Bridges & Tunnels Remained Stable

* Minimal decline (~6%)
* Demand remained relatively consistent

👉 **Insight:**
Shift toward private vehicle usage during and after COVID.

---

### 7. 📅 Weekdays Still Lead Ridership

* Weekday: **~43%**
* Holiday: **~30%**
* Weekend: **~26%**

👉 **Insight:**
Work-related travel remains the primary driver of ridership.

---

### 8. 🔄 Behavioral Shift in Travel Patterns

* Decrease in weekday dominance compared to pre-COVID
* Increase in weekend and holiday usage

👉 **Insight:**
Transportation is increasingly used for leisure rather than commuting.

---

### 9. 📈 Recovery Plateau After 2023

* Most systems peaked in 2023
* Slight stabilization or decline in 2024

👉 **Insight:**
The system has reached a **"new normal"** rather than full recovery.

---

### 10. 📉 Strong Seasonality in Monthly Trends

* Sharp drop in 2020
* Gradual recovery with fluctuations
* Certain months recover faster than others

👉 **Insight:**
Ridership is influenced by seasonality and external events.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

### 1. Optimize Weekday Operations

* Adjust capacity based on reduced commuting demand
* Focus on peak-hour efficiency

### 2. Invest in Subway Infrastructure

* As the core system, improvements here yield the highest impact

### 3. Improve Bus Network Strategy

* Re-evaluate routes and frequency
* Address declining demand trends

### 4. Adapt to Behavioral Changes

* Enhance weekend and off-peak services
* Support leisure-based travel patterns

### 5. Encourage Public Transport Usage

* Counter the shift toward private vehicles
* Improve reliability and user experience

---

## 🧾 Conclusion

The COVID-19 pandemic did not just disrupt ridership temporarily—it fundamentally reshaped commuter behavior.

The NYC transportation system is now operating under a **new equilibrium**, where:

* Remote work influences demand
* Leisure travel plays a larger role
* Recovery varies significantly by transport mode

Understanding these patterns is essential for making **data-driven decisions** and building a more resilient transportation system.
